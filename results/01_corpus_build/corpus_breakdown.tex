\section{Identity verification and human review}

Automated verification compared each PDF's first two pages against its Zotero metadata. Of 2063 papers,
2041 verified automatically (1989 on DOI, 52 on title$+$author) and 24 were flagged for a human. A further
4 were flagged later by title screening and thin-text detection.

\begin{table}[h]
\centering

\midrule
\textbf{Total reviewed by hand} & \textbf{21} & \textbf{7} \\
\bottomrule
\end{tabular}
\end{table}

All 28 queued papers are decided; none remain outstanding. Replacements span 14 institute folders, the
largest being NIMH (4), NCI HLS (3), and NICHD (2). Twelve of the originally flagged documents were
NIH grant summary statements or study-section review sheets filed where the article should be --- caught
by a header-phrase rule, since a grant and the paper it funds share a title and first author.

The 4 correction notices were 2 flagged by character count (a Corrigendum and an Erratum) and 2 by a
title-anchored rule matching seven markers (\emph{erratum}, \emph{corrigendum}, \emph{correction},
\emph{retraction}, \emph{withdrawn}, \emph{addendum}, \emph{expression of concern}). Anchoring to the
title's start is required: unanchored matching would also have removed two genuine trials, one on
``Reentry from Corrections to Community Treatment'' and one on ``Neonatal Opioid Withdrawal''. For 3 of
the 4 notices the corrected article is independently present in the corpus; only the corrigendum to
``Analysis of cluster-randomized test-negative designs'' has no underlying article on file.

No PDF in the corpus lacked a text layer, so OCR was never invoked. Extraction is text-only: tables
linearize into label/value runs that preserve reading order and arm labels, while figure content survives
only as embedded text. Cached text is invalidated by the source PDF's md5, so replacing a PDF during
review forces re-extraction. \textbf{The extracted text has not yet been checked for encoding corruption,
multi-article PDFs, or truncation.}

The 347 excluded papers carry reasons dominated by \emph{secondary analysis} (164), followed by
\emph{implementation} (31), \emph{duplicate study by the same group} (31), \emph{methods paper} (30),
\emph{not group-randomized} (23), and \emph{baseline analysis} (23); the remaining nine categories
each account for fewer than a dozen. NCI's review additionally sorts its 95 gate survivors into
review categories A (30), B (19), C (33), and D (13) --- NHLBI's review does not use this scheme, so
it is blank on 81 of the 176 kept papers.

\textbf{15 papers were reviewed independently by both institutes}, because they were fetched into
both Zotero groups. 9 of the 15 pairs agree on every field and were merged automatically; 6 disagree
and neither answer is trusted over the other. One disagreement is a complete flip --- NCI calls a
paper's power and data analysis both correct, NHLBI calls the same paper's analyses both incorrect.
All 6 are listed with each institute's answer in \texttt{results/review/05\_label\_match\_review.csv},
awaiting a human decision.
