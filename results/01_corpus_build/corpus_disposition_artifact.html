What gets measured

Three tasks, and why accuracy is the wrong number

Inclusion was dropped: every paper in this study is already included, and nothing in the labels encodes it.

TaskPapersClass splitScore for always guessing the majority
Exclusion492316 exclude / 176 keep64%
Power analysis176136 incorrect / 40 correct77%
Data analysis176129 incorrect / 47 correct73%

A model that answers "incorrect" every time scores 77%

That is the problem with accuracy on imbalanced data: the number looks respectable and means nothing. Three fixes, all of which belong in the methods section — report balanced accuracy or macro-F1 as the headline, since both collapse to 50% for a constant guesser; print the majority-class baseline beside every figure so a reader can see the floor; and give the full confusion matrix, because for exclusion a wrong keep is cheap and a wrong exclude removes a paper from the study permanently.

And because this compares a model against human reviewers rather than against objective truth — the source paper calls its own labels "the opinion of two knowledgeable reviewers, rather than an objective classification scheme" — Cohen's κ is the right statistic, read against the 80% the two institutes managed with each other.
